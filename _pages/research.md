## Approach

I am interested in how **coherent structures** (vortices, fronts, filaments, boundary layers, convective structures) form in rotating and stratified fluids, how they evolve, and how they interact with their surroundings: stratification, the background flow, and exchanges across scales.

My approach is process-oriented: I try to identify the mechanism behind the emergence or transformation of a structure, and the exchanges of energy and momentum involved. I then test that understanding against experiments, observations, and numerical or analytical models. I am most drawn to problems where an experiment or a simulation lets you follow a process directly, and a simple analytical description then explains how it works.

## Postdoc: University of St Andrews

I am a Research Fellow in the Vortex Dynamics Group (School of Mathematics and Statistics, University of St Andrews), working with Daphné Lemasquerier. I am building a new rotating-table experiment with two strands:

- **Zonal jets and topography.** How jets in a rotating fluid interact with bottom topography.
- **Horizontal centrifugal convection.** Convection driven horizontally by the centrifugal force on a rotating table.

This project takes me beyond mixed layers and boundary layers, towards the dynamics, instabilities and organisation of coherent structures in geophysical flows. It covers the full experimental cycle: designing the set-up, running the lab measurements, analysing the physics and modelling it.

## PhD: wind- and convection-driven ocean mixing

My PhD at Université Grenoble Alpes (IGE & LEGI), supervised by Joël Sommeria and Bruno Deremble, looked at how the **ocean mixed layer** develops under wind and convection. It focused on how a surface forcing generates mixing and penetrates into the ocean, and how **Earth's rotation** changes that process.

- **From turbulent mixing to Ekman dynamics.** The transition between regimes dominated by turbulent mixing and regimes where rotation imposes an Ekman-type dynamics, and the role of **inertial oscillations** in the ocean's transient response.
- **Transient turbulent Ekman layers.** How such a layer develops, how it is damped, and how a friction applied at the surface is transmitted into the fluid interior.
- **Turbulence closures.** Using and validating closure models for ocean models. One key result: with an appropriate representation of friction, a model reproduces both the evolution of the Ekman layer and the damping of the inertial oscillations.

The work combined experiments on the 13 m Coriolis rotating platform (LEGI), one-dimensional numerical models and theory ([publications](/publication/), [thesis manuscript](/file/Manuscrit_these_CPN_compressed.pdf)).
